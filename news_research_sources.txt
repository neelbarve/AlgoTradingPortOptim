NEWS AND FX PRICE MOVEMENT - SOURCES
Prepared 25 Nov 2026 for the FX Strategy Portfolio project.

How these were used: every source below was found through web searches. I worked from the
search results' abstracts, publisher pages and summaries; I did not read the full text of the
papers (several are paywalled). Check exact figures against the original papers before citing
them in the report.


======================================================================
PART 1 - SOURCES CITED IN THE ANSWER
======================================================================

1. Andersen, T., Bollerslev, T., Diebold, F. X. and Vega, C. (2003).
   "Micro Effects of Macro Announcements: Real-Time Price Discovery in Foreign Exchange."
   American Economic Review 93(1), 38-62.
   Used for: only surprises move prices; bad news has a bigger impact than good news.
   - https://www.nber.org/papers/w8959
   - https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=313660
   - https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/currency_exchange_rates   (Kellogg Insight summary)

2. Ederington, L. H. and Lee, J. H. (1993).
   "How Markets Process Information: News Releases and Volatility."
   Journal of Finance 48(4), 1161-1191.
   Used for: adjustment within the first minute; volatility elevated about 15 minutes;
   scheduled releases explain most time-of-day and day-of-week volatility patterns.
   - https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v48y1993i4p1161-91.html

3. Evans, M. D. D. and Lyons, R. K. (2008).
   "How Is Macro News Transmitted to Exchange Rates?"
   Journal of Financial Economics 88(1), 26-50.
   Used for: direct effect of scheduled news < 10% of daily variance; news overall > 30%;
   about two-thirds transmitted through order flow.
   - https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jfinec:v:88:y:2008:i:1:p:26-50
   - https://www.nber.org/papers/w9433   (working paper version)

4. Evans, M. D. D. and Lyons, R. K. (2002).
   "Order Flow and Exchange Rate Dynamics."
   Journal of Political Economy 110(1), 170-180.
   Used for: order flow explains more than 60% of daily DM/$ changes.
   - https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:ucp:jpolec:v:110:y:2002:i:1:p:170-180
   - https://nber.org/papers/w7317   (working paper version)

5. Meese, R. A. and Rogoff, K. (1983).
   "Empirical Exchange Rate Models of the Seventies: Do They Fit Out of Sample?"
   Journal of International Economics 14(1-2), 3-24.
   Used for: fundamentals-based models do not beat a random walk out of sample.
   - https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~behansen/390/MeeseRogoff.pdf

6. Goldberg, L. and Grisse, C. (2013).
   "Time Variation in Asset Price Responses to Macro Announcements."
   Federal Reserve Bank of New York Staff Report 626 (also NBER w19523).
   Used for: the importance of a release changes over time with policy rates and risk conditions.
   - https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/staff_reports/sr626.pdf

7. Rosa, C. (2011).
   "The High-Frequency Response of Exchange Rates to Monetary Policy Actions and Statements."
   Journal of Banking and Finance 35(2), 478-489.
   Used for: Fed decisions and statements have large effects; statement surprises explain
   most of the move; absorbed in 30-40 minutes.
   - https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jbfina:v:35:y:2011:i:2:p:478-489

8. Cheung, Y.-W. and Chinn, M. D. (2001).
   "Currency Traders and Exchange Rate Dynamics: A Survey of the US Market."
   Journal of International Money and Finance 20(4), 439-471.
   Used for: interest rates always important to traders; importance of other variables shifts;
   traders watch the trade balance.
   - https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:jimfin:v:20:y:2001:i:4:p:439-471

9. Ederington, L. H., Guan, W. and Yang, L. (2019).
   "The Impact of the U.S. Employment Report on Exchange Rates."
   Journal of International Money and Finance 90, 257-267.
   Used for: nonfarm payrolls is the most important US release for EUR, GBP and JPY;
   revisions and the unemployment rate also matter.
   - https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jimfin/v90y2019icp257-267.html

10. CXO Advisory. "Which Economic Data Announcements Matter?"
    Summary of an intraday study covering 1998-2007.
    Used for: payrolls, GDP advance and ISM manufacturing were the releases with large,
    lasting price responses. (Secondary summary, not the original paper.)
    - https://cxoadvisory.com/economic-indicators/which-economic-data-announcements-matter

11. Filippou, I., Taylor, M. P. and Wang, G. (2023).
    "Media Sentiment and Currency Reversals."
    Journal of Financial and Quantitative Analysis.
    Used for: high media sentiment predicts lower currency returns; long-low / short-high
    sentiment strategy over 48 currencies and 35 years.
    - https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/media-sentiment-and-currency-reversals/84CEB4F2EEE1521C3C694F547AC35A0B

12. Neely, C. J. and Dey, S. R. (2010).
    "A Survey of Announcement Effects on Foreign Exchange Returns."
    Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis Review 92(5), 417-464.
    Recommended as the starting survey.
    - https://ideas.repec.org/a/fip/fedlrv/y2010isepp417-464nv.92no.5.html
